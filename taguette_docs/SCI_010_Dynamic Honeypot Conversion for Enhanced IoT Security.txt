─── JUSTIFICATION TEXT (for coding) ───

[DOMAIN]
Value: IoT
Evidence: Page 1: The title and abstract focus exclusively on the "Internet of Things (IoT)"

[INTERACTION LEVEL]
Evidence: Page 3: The authors argue that "high-interaction honeypots... are impractical for widespread use in IoT". They advocate for a system that balances "realistic interaction" while controlling risk, which aligns with their definition of Medium-interaction

[THREAT MODEL]
Value: IoT malware, automated attacks, and opportunistic attackers
Evidence: Page 5: The model assumes an attacker "capable of launching various attacks targeting IoT nodes," including "volumetric attacks, protocol attacks, and application-layer attacks"

[IRB / ETHICS COMMITTEE]
Evidence: Pages 1–15: There is no mention of an "Institutional Review Board," "ethics committee," or "human subject protocol" in the entire paper.

[CONSENT MODEL]
Evidence: Page 5: The attacker operates under the limitation that they "cannot determine whether a node is a honeypot before interaction" and remain "unaware of the system’s dynamic conversion capability," implying data is collected without their knowledge or consent

[DATA TYPES COLLECTED]
Evidence: Page 7: The system records security events defined by Equation (21) as: "e = (timestamp, event type, node id, data)," where 'data' represents the interaction content

[PROTOCOLS & PORTS]
Evidence: Page 8:
The CICDDoS2019 dataset provides diverse attack patterns
relevant to IoT environments: volumetric attacks (UDP flood,
DNS amplification), protocol attacks (SYN flood, ICMP flood),
and application-layer attacks (HTTP flood, Slowloris).

[DATA SENSITIVITY]
Value: Medium
Evidence: Categorized as Medium because the recorded "data" variable captures specific attack payloads and interaction behaviors from the CICDDoS2019 dataset rather than personal human identifiers or user credentials

[DATA MINIMIZATION]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Page 5:
The paper does not mention the "minimization principle" or filtering specific data fields; instead, it focuses on "Evidence Preservation" through immutable logging

[DATA RETENTION POLICY]
Evidence: Page 2:
While the blockchain component ensures "immutable records" of conversion events and attacks, there is no discussion of a deletion timeline or storage expiration policy

[DATA SHARING]
Value: None mentioned
Evidence: Page 8:
The authors provide a GitHub link for the implementation source code, but they do not mention a public release for the raw interaction traces or attacker logs

[JURISDICTION / LEGAL]
Evidence: There is no analysis of GDPR, legal compliance for node conversion, or jurisdictional considerations for the deployment

[CONTAINMENT & HARM]
Evidence: The system initiates a (Page 2) "rapid isolation protocol" and transitions nodes through specific phases (normal→isolating→converting→operational) to (Page 4) "maintain network stability and prevent data loss"

[DECEPTION FRAMING]
Evidence: Page 2:
	
Deception is treated as a functional requirement to provide "greater flexibility and resource efficiency" and realistic interaction in defense

[RESEARCHER RESPONSIBILITY]
Value: Implicit (Technical safety)
Evidence: Page 5:
Responsibility is implicit through the system's "Network Resilience" constraints, which mandate a "minimum 40% normal node availability" to ensure legitimate network functionality during attacks

[ETHICAL GAPS]
Value: Ethics-by-Design without formal governance
Evidence: Like the vast majority of papers in the study, it relies on technical containment (isolation protocols and state machines) as a substitute for formal ethical discussion or IRB oversight

[RELEVANCE TO PROJECT]
Evidence: BHICS is a primary example of the "technology-driven ethical lag." It uses a sophisticated Digital Twin concept (converting real nodes into high-fidelity decoys) but lacks the IRB oversight found in HoneySat

[DEPLOYMENT]
Value: Experimental / controlled
Evidence: Section 5.4 describes an experimental setup that "simulates an IoT network comprising 100 nodes," while Section 7.5 lists "implementing a real-world testbed" as a "future research priority"
