[DOMAIN]
Value: IoT
Evidence: Title: Focuses on "IoT Botnet Lifecycle Detection." Section I: Addresses "the security of smart terminals in edge computing... including botnets"

[INTERACTION LEVEL]
Value: Medium
Evidence: Page 11:
Paper never self-classifies interaction level. Architecture is request-response based: "the IoT botnet sends a request... and awaits a response. If the response conforms to the expected format... recorded as a successful interaction" 
(Section V-B-2a). No real command execution, shell access, or persistent attacker state is described  closer to medium-interaction than full system emulation.

[THREAT MODEL]
Value: IoT botnets (Scanning to Attack phases)
Evidence: Abstract: Targets the "botnet scanning and propagation phases". Section III: Classifies the lifecycle into "scanning phase, propagation phase, and attack phase"

[IRB / ETHICS COMMITTEE]
Value: No
Evidence: Pages 1–16: A review of the entire manuscript confirms no mention of an "Institutional Review Board," "ethics committee," or human subject protocols

[CONSENT MODEL]
Value: Implicit
Evidence: Section I: The system is designed to "trap attackers"
 and "deceive potential attackers"
 who interact with the decoy unaware of its nature.

[DATA TYPES COLLECTED]
Value: Synthetic/simulated IoT protocol interactions generated by researcher-deployed Mirai sample and custom brute-force dictionary (20 passwords); no real attacker data captured
Evidence: Page 9:
"VM1 (Simulated IoT Botnet): VM1 hosts a simulated IoT botnet."
Page 10:
"In this particular experiment, “root” is the chosen username, granting superuser privileges. Subsequently, a password is randomly selected from a dictionary of common passwords (comprising 20 entries)"
Page 11:
"Mirai is deployed as the botnet attacker" (a lab-deployed Mirai sample, not a live real-world attacker)

[PROTOCOLS & PORTS]
Value: Telnet, HTTP, SSH, MQTT, CoAP
Evidence: Table V / Section V-B: Lists protocols used for training/testing including HTTP, DNS, DHCP, Telnet, SSL, SSH. 

Section V-B-1: Explicitly mentions evaluating MQTT, CoAP, HTTP, and SSH

[DATA SENSITIVITY]
Value: Low
Evidence: Section V-A-1: "VM1 (Simulated IoT Botnet): VM1 hosts a simulated IoT botnet." Section V-B-1(b): "'root' is the chosen username... a password is randomly selected from a dictionary of common passwords (comprising 20 entries) for the brute-force attack." All experiments (Sections V-A to V-B) were conducted in an isolated 6-VM VMWare testbed with no internet exposure. The "credentials," malware, and C2 traffic captured are entirely synthetic generated by a researcher-deployed Mirai sample interacting with a GAN-based response generator, not real attacker data. No real human, attacker, or third-party data was collected at any point.

[DATA MINIMIZATION]

[ETHICAL GAPS]
Value: Structural Ethical Vacuum
Evidence: Section I, Section IV, and Section V-A-1: 
The system utilizes GANs to generate deceptive responses while relying solely on a "virtual testbed" and the "VMWare platform" for safety. The paper contains no mention of an IRB, legal analysis, or dual-use discussion. 
This follows the project's meta-pattern where "technical safety (containment) replaces normative reasoning" 

Unlike most dataset entries, RGPot involved zero real-world internet exposure and zero real human/attacker data collection  entirely simulated in an isolated 6-VM VMWare testbed. This substantially lowers actual privacy/harm risk compared to internet-facing honeypots, though the paper still provides no formal ethics framework.
