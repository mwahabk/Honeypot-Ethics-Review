─── JUSTIFICATION TEXT (for coding) ───

[DOMAIN]
Value: CPS
Evidence: Index Terms & Abstract: Explicitly targets the "Internet of Things (IoT)" and discusses vulnerabilities in devices used in Healthcare, Smart Homes, and Autonomous Vehicles.

[INTERACTION LEVEL]
Evidence: Page 2: The authors describe a new category beyond traditional levels called "intelligent-interaction honeypots based on ML," designed to maximize the likelihood of catching attacks rather than just emulating a specific service.

[THREAT MODEL]
Value: IoT attackers & scanners
Evidence: Section I: Targets adversaries performing a "pre-check attack step" where they scan for open ports and perform checks on the target device before launching the "exploit-code".

[IRB / ETHICS COMMITTEE]
Evidence: A complete review of the paper confirms there is no mention of an Institutional Review Board (IRB), ethics committee, or institutional approval for studying attacker interactions.

[CONSENT MODEL]
Evidence: Sections I& III & IV: The AIIPot system uses an AI-driven "honey-chatbot" (BERT/MDP) to lure attackers into interactions that mimic a "real fashion" and effectively "bypass" their "pre-check attack" steps. 
To maximize deception depth, it employs a novel "OOV" request technique that broadcasts unknown attacker commands to a local network to retrieve and replay valid responses. This high-fidelity manipulation allowed the researchers to capture exploit code from "987 different IP addresses" on the public Internet without providing any ethical disclosure or obtaining consent.

[DATA TYPES COLLECTED]
Evidence: Section III & IV: 
Captures "behaviour of attackers" through a "req/res database" that logs unique "IP addresses" (987 observed) and specific interaction traces to extract the "malicious payload".

[PROTOCOLS & PORTS]
Evidence: Section IV: 
The evaluation was "carried out only on the HTTP protocol dataset" and deployed on a "GCP web server," implying standard web ports (80/443).

[DATA SENSITIVITY]
Value: High
Evidence: Section III-B: 
The honeypot collects "malicious payloads" and "exploit code" from attackers, which are categorized as high-sensitivity threat intelligence artifacts.

[DATA MINIMIZATION]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Section III-A: 
The system is designed to broadcast new requests to the local network to find responses and save them to the database, with no stated policy on filtering or limiting the scope of collected data.

[DATA RETENTION POLICY]
Evidence: Section III-A: Mentions that all datasets are "saved on the req/res database," but the paper provides no policy regarding storage duration or deletion.

[DATA SHARING]
Value: Not mentioned
Evidence: Section IV: While the authors use existing datasets (FirmPot and NSL-KDD), they do not provide a public link or repository for the new interaction logs or exploit code captured during their GCP deployment.

[JURISDICTION / LEGAL]
Evidence: Section IV: The system was deployed on the "Google Cloud Platform (GCP)" by authors from Cameroon, the UK, and the USA, but the paper contains no discussion of legal compliance or cross-border data regulations.

[CONTAINMENT & HARM]
Evidence: Section III-A: Safety is managed through an "evaluator module" and a "Honey-chatbot" server that assess the trustworthiness of requests, though the primary defense is the use of emulated rather than physical devices.

[DECEPTION FRAMING]
Evidence: Section III-B: 
Deception is framed as a technical goal to make the attacker "believe our honeypot is the vulnerable target IoT device" using a "probabilistic trade-off between exploration, reply with responses which have not been used before, and exploitation".

[RESEARCHER RESPONSIBILITY]
Value: Implicit
Evidence: Section I & Abstract: 
Responsibility is framed as a contribution to "improving the security posture of IoT systems" by helping organizations detect and monitor attack tactics.

[ETHICAL GAPS]
Value: Structural Ethical Vacuum
Evidence: Page 6:
The paper exemplifies the project's "structural ethical vacuum" finding. Despite being "sponsored by the Army Research Office", it involves the AI-driven manipulation of human attackers without any IRB oversight or data governance framework.

[RELEVANCE TO PROJECT]
Evidence: Section III-B: This serves as a primary case study for ethical lag. 
It uses advanced AI "BERT" /RL to increase deception depth and session length  while remaining entirely silent on the normative ethical implications of such advanced manipulation.

[DEPLOYMENT]
Value: Yes (Internet-facing)
Evidence: Section IV: 
The system was deployed on a "public server on the Google Cloud Platform (GCP)" for 20 days, where it captured 6,235 requests from 987 different IP addresses.
