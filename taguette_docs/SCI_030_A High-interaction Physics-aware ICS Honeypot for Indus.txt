─── JUSTIFICATION TEXT (for coding) ───

[DOMAIN]
Value: ICS
Evidence: Abstract & Keywords: Explicitly targets "Industrial Control Systems (ICS)" and "SCADA" environments, focusing on the protection of critical infrastructure like water and power supply.

[INTERACTION LEVEL]
Value: High
Evidence: Table I: The authors categorize their honeypot as "High" interaction because it includes a simulated operating system and a "complete physical simulation of an ICS."

[THREAT MODEL]
Value: ICS/SCADA Attackers
Evidence: the model addresses adversaries performing "reconnaissance" using tools like Nmap and Shodan, as well as those attempting "PLC" manipulation. 
The system is uniquely designed to capture and analyze "malicious ladder logic programs" and unauthorized "code injection attempts" targeting the S7comm protocol. 
Furthermore, it accounts for attackers who use "crafted Modbus holding register write request" to manipulate physical process parameters, such as "chlorine release level" and "valve opening level," in an attempt to cause real-world industrial damage.

[IRB / ETHICS COMMITTEE]
Value: No
Evidence: A complete review confirms no mention of an Institutional Review Board (IRB), ethics committee, or formal oversight for studying attacker behavior

[CONSENT MODEL]
Value: None
Evidence: Section IV-A (actual experiment, not the proposed architecture in Section II-B): testing occurred entirely within an isolated "virtual network environment... using a GNS3 network emulator" with a simulated "Client 1... legitimate client" and "Client 2... attacker" (Section III-E). No real internet exposure or real attacker interaction occurred in this study, the VPN-exposed production deployment described in Section II-B is a proposed future architecture, not what was actually tested.

[DATA TYPES COLLECTED]
Value: IPs, protocol logs, and ladder logic
Evidence: Section III-D & V: The system logs interaction traces for "HTTP, SNMP, Modbus and S7comm" and uses Snort IDS to record "S7comm functions such as reading memory blocks and code injection attempts."

[PROTOCOLS & PORTS]
Value: S7comm (102), HTTP (80), Modbus (502)
Evidence: Section III-A & Fig. 2: Specifically redirects traffic from ports 102 (S7comm), 80 (HTTP), and 502 (Modbus) to its simulated servers.

[DATA SENSITIVITY]
Value: Low
Evidence: Section IV-A: "The experiment setup consists of three virtual machines... HoneyPLC++ is deployed in a virtual machine, HoneySim++ is deployed in another virtual machine that is only accessible from HoneyPLC++." Section III-E: "Client 1 is assumed as a legitimate client, and client 2 is assumed as an attacker" both are simulated by researchers, not real adversaries. Section IV-E confirms: "The system was tested by increasing the chlorine release level to 65000" this was researcher-conducted testing, not real attacker interaction. No real-world deployment or real attacker data was ever collected.

[DATA MINIMIZATION]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Section I-A: The paper states that high-interaction honeypots are used for "collecting large amounts of information," with no stated policy on filtering or reducing the scope of data.

[DATA RETENTION POLICY]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Section III-D: 
Mentions that researchers can identify events and "go through the necessary logs," but provides no policy regarding storage duration or deletion.

[DATA SHARING]
Value: None mentioned
Evidence: Although published as an "open-access article" under the Creative Commons license, the paper provides no public link or repository for the HoneyPLC++ source code, the HoneySim++ physics-aware simulation, or the specific interaction traces captured. 
This lack of sharing reflects the identified "structural ethical vacuum," where sensitive industrial telemetry including "malicious ladder logic" and "valve opening level" is collected without defined policies for public access or responsible disclosure

[JURISDICTION / LEGAL]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Page 1:
Despite authors being from the "University of Moratuwa", Sri Lanka, there is no discussion of legal compliance, jurisdiction, or cross-border data transfer laws

[CONTAINMENT & HARM]
Value: Technical isolation & Fail-safes
Evidence: Section III-E & IV-E: 
Safety is achieved through a "virtualized deployment environment" and "basic fail-safe mechanisms" in HoneySim++ (e.g., pumps automatically switching off if outlet valves are closed).

[DECEPTION FRAMING]
Value: Purely technical
Evidence: Section I & II-A: 
Deception is framed as an engineering challenge to achieve "physics awareness" the ability to replicate "timing delays" and "actual physical behaviour" to avoid detection by expert attackers.

[RESEARCHER RESPONSIBILITY]
Value: Implicit
Evidence: Section III-D & IV-F (Page 13, 19): Responsibility is framed as a contribution to helping "security personnel" identify "new attack vectors" and safeguard real ICS.

[ETHICAL GAPS]
Value: Structural Ethical Vacuum
Evidence: the research captures sensitive industrial commands and attacker identities without IRB oversight or a data governance framework

[RELEVANCE TO PROJECT]
Value: Ethics-by-Containment Model
Evidence: Sections II-A & IV-E:
The authors implement HoneySim++ to authentically replicate "actual physical behaviuor,"  justifying a "virtualized deployment" with "fail-safe mechanisms" as a cheaper and safer alternative to real systems. 
This exemplifies the "ethics-by-containment" model where technical isolation serves as a complete substitute for normative ethical reasoning. 
Operating in a structural ethical vacuum, the research lacks the IRB oversight found in HoneySat despite capturing high-sensitivity data like "malicious ladder logic" and the manipulation of "chlorine release level" . 
Its high-fidelity, physics-aware simulation makes it a primary case study for Digital Twin ethical analysis.

[DEPLOYMENT]
Value: Experimental Prototype
Evidence: Section III-E: Evaluated in a "virtual network environment" using the GNS3 network emulator and virtual machines, rather than a live, persistent Internet deployment.
