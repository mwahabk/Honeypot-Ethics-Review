─── JUSTIFICATION TEXT (for coding) ───

[DOMAIN]
Value: Space
Evidence: Page 1: The abstract explicitly identifies the domain as "satellites," specifically focusing on "Small Satellite (SmallSat)" and "CubeSat" missions

[INTERACTION LEVEL]
Value: High
Evidence: Page 1: The authors define the system as a "high-interaction satellite honeypot framework" and compare it to others in Table I as a "High" interaction system

[THREAT MODEL]
Value: Network-based attackers targeting satellite communication services
Evidence: Page 1: The framework is designed "to attract and analyze adversaries who attack space infrastructures over the Internet, a commonly observed threat vector"

[IRB / ETHICS COMMITTEE]
Value: Yes
Evidence: Section IX explicitly states, "the survey protocol, and materials were submitted for review by our Institutional Review Board (IRB)"

[CONSENT MODEL]
Value: Implicit
Evidence: Page14: 
For the deployment, the authors use "notice and warning" banners (authorized use only) rather than explicit consent, as "trespassers... do not have reasonable expectation of privacy"

[DATA TYPES COLLECTED]
Value: Network traffic, Attack attempts, Protocol interaction data
Evidence: Page 5: The logging repository records "TM/TC traffic to and from the ground station software and the logging attempts received in the web interface"

[PROTOCOLS & PORTS]
Value: Telnet (TCP 23), Web Interface (HTTP, TCP 80), VNC (TCP 5901), ZeroMQ (CSP Network raw packet access)
Evidence: Page10:
D. Internet Interaction Experiment

Page 7:
We implemented a VNC
server, a Telnet server, a Web server, and ZeroMQ-based
access to the CSP Network"

[DATA SENSITIVITY]
Value: High
Evidence: Page 3: The system captures "real-world adversarial interactions"
 including "telecommands" (TC) that, if valid, allow an attacker to "fully take over the mission"

[DATA MINIMIZATION]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Pages 1-14: While privacy for survey participants is addressed
, there is no discussion of the "minimization principle" regarding the scope of adversarial data captured

[DATA RETENTION POLICY]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Page 7: The implementation uses a "MongoDB database
 to aggregate and store logs," but no retention or deletion timeline is defined

[DATA SHARING]
Value: Source code and Dockerized artifact publicly released (Zenodo + GitHub); raw interaction logs not released
Evidence: Page 2: While "source code and experimental results are available online", there is no specific mention of a public release policy for the raw interaction datasets

Zenodo record confirms full artifact release at 
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17871431

[JURISDICTION / LEGAL]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Page 10: The paper mentions deployment in "Germany and the United States"
 but does not include a legal analysis or mention of GDPR compliance

[CONTAINMENT & HARM]
Value: Dual-layer sandboxing (Docker + VM)
Evidence: Page 14: Risk is mitigated by deploying "using two sandboxing layers (Docker containers and Virtual Machines (VMs)" on an isolated network segment

[DECEPTION FRAMING]
Value: Ethical Discussion
Evidence: Page 4: Deception is justified as a technical design objective (DO-2)
 and ethically as a necessary tool to "entice adversaries" while notifying them of authorized use only

[RESEARCHER RESPONSIBILITY]
Value: Explicit (Focus on avoiding harm rather 
than broader ethical theory)
Evidence: Section IX is entirely dedicated to "Ethical Considerations," specifically identifying stakeholders and outlining mitigation strategies for both participants and actors

[ETHICAL GAPS]
Value: No formal framework beyond harm prevention
Evidence: Page 14: While it includes an ethics section, the paper lacks a "formal ethical framework" or discussion of "governance" and "legality" beyond immediate harm prevention

[RELEVANCE TO PROJECT]
Value: Strong example of implicit ethics plus brief explicit acknowledgment, Useful 
contrast with papers that omit ethics sections entirely
Evidence: Page 14: This is the only paper in the study that explicitly discusses IRB approval
, providing the necessary contrast to the "ethical vacuum" found in the rest of the literature

[DEPLOYMENT]
Value: Yes (Public Internet)
Evidence: Table 4: The authors "deployed five instances of HoneySat over the Internet" across on-premises servers in Chile and cloud servers in Germany and the USA
