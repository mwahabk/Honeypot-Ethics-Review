[DOMAIN]
Value: CPS
Evidence: The paper proposes a Cyber-Physical Deception System (CPDS) that coordinates multiple IoT honeypots to mimic cyber-physical dependencies among IoT devices, making honeypots undetectable by cross-layer attackers. It is explicitly a honeypot design and deployment paper for IoT/CPS environments.

[INTERACTION LEVEL]
Evidence: CPDS coordinates multiple honeypots with modeled vulnerabilities and physical/network dependency rules. Attackers can interact with services and receive consistent cross-layer responses  higher than low-interaction but not full system access, making this medium interaction.

[THREAT MODEL]
Value: IoT device attackers operating across both cyber (network exploitation) and physical (device state verification) domains, capable of detecting inconsistencies among honeypots through multi-source attacks.
Evidence: Abstract: "attackers can easily detect inconsistencies among the honeypots after launching attacks from multiple sources, spanning both cyber and physical domains, to verify device status."

[IRB / ETHICS COMMITTEE]
Evidence: No mention of IRB, ethics committee, institutional approval, or human subjects protocol in any section of the paper.

[CONSENT MODEL]
Evidence: Honeypots are deployed facing real-world attackers. No consent or disclosure mechanism is described. Attackers are treated implicitly as adversaries whose interactions are logged without notification.

[DATA TYPES COLLECTED]
Evidence: Paper describes collecting and analyzing "attack traces" to model device vulnerabilities. CVE identifiers (CVE-2020-12109, CVE-2021-44402, CVE-2018-3928, CVE-2019-3949, CVE-2020-35577) are referenced as attack data collected.

[PROTOCOLS & PORTS]
Evidence: Related work section references U-POT (UPnP protocol), and the system emulates protocol-level responses for various IoT device types. Specific ports depend on emulated device class.

[DATA SENSITIVITY]

[DECEPTION FRAMING]
