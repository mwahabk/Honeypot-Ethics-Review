[DOMAIN]
Value: ICS
Evidence: Abstract: Explicitly focuses on "Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs)," which are "essential components of industrial control systems (ICS)"

[INTERACTION LEVEL]
Value: Medium
Evidence: Section 3.3/3.3.1: Classified as "Medium" interaction because it uses "packet replay techniques" and delivers responses "without executing corresponding actions," acting as a protocol-level proxy rather than a full system simulation

[THREAT MODEL]
Value: PLC-targeted attacks
Evidence: Section 5.2: Targets adversaries performing "control logic injection," "reconnaissance (control logic theft)," and "brute force authentication"

[IRB / ETHICS COMMITTEE]
Value: No
Evidence: There is no mention of an Institutional Review Board or ethical committee approval, despite federal funding from the "National Science Foundation"

[CONSENT MODEL]
Value: Implicit
Evidence: Sections 3.3.2 & 4.3: The system uses a "Data Storage" module to capture all interactions from adversaries who are "deceive attacker" without being notified of the decoy

[DATA TYPES COLLECTED]
Value: IPs, protocol dialogues, hashes, and binaries
Evidence: Sections 3.3.2 & 4.3/5.3: Specifically records "all communication" in a Data Storage module, including "attacker’s password hash" and "control logic binaries"

[PROTOCOLS & PORTS]
Value: Modbus (502)
Evidence: Section 3.3.1: The framework emulates Modbus/TCP on "Port 502" for the Modicon M221 and uses replay to handle other proprietary protocols like PCCC

[DATA SENSITIVITY]
Value: Low
Evidence: All captured data (including "the attacker's password hash" in Section 4.3) originates from researcher-executed test attacks in an isolated lab environment, not real attackers or production systems.

[DATA MINIMIZATION]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Section 3.3: The paper emphasizes preserving "all communication" for forensic analysis and does not mention any filtering or anonymization practices

[DATA RETENTION POLICY]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Section 3.3: While the architecture includes a "Data Storage" module, the paper provides no policy on storage duration or data deletion

[DATA SHARING]
Value: Not mentioned
Evidence: Unlike other sources, the text does not provide a GitHub link or public dataset repository for the framework’s code or collected logs

[JURISDICTION / LEGAL]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Despite gathering sensitive threat intelligence, the paper contains no discussion of legal compliance, jurisdiction, or international data laws

[CONTAINMENT & HARM]
Value: Replay-based emulation
Evidence: Section 6: Safety is achieved technically because the system "is not connected to a real physical system" and lacks "real-time operational dynamics" that could cause harm

[DECEPTION FRAMING]
Value: Purely technical
Evidence: Section 3.3.2 and 5: Deception is framed as a technical goal to "mimic a real PLC" and "deceive attackers convincingly" through protocol-level accuracy

[RESEARCHER RESPONSIBILITY]
Value: Implicit
Evidence: Sections 1 & 7: Responsibility is framed as a contribution to "threat intelligence" and the security community through a "scalable" emulation approach

[ETHICAL GAPS]
Value: Structural Ethical Vacuum
Evidence: Reflects the project’s meta-finding of an "ethics-by-containment" model; it collects sensitive credentials without IRB oversight or a data governance policy

[RELEVANCE TO PROJECT]
Value: Realism-focused deception
Evidence: Sec. 7: Primary example of prioritizing "realism" and application functionalities over normative ethical considerations

[DEPLOYMENT]
Value: No (Lab/Experimental only)
Evidence: Section 4.1 and Section 5: All evaluation conducted on "Windows 10 VM (engineering workstation)" and "Ubuntu 18 VM" (PLCpot) on the same isolated network. Case study attacks were self-executed by researchers on a separate Ubuntu 20 VM, not real-world attacker traffic.
