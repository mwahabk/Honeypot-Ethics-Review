[DOMAIN]
Value: IT
Evidence: Abstract & Sec. VIII: Targets "SSH honeypot" and emulates a "Linux shell environment" to engage attackers

[INTERACTION LEVEL]
Value: Medium
Evidence: Sec. I & II: Aimed at providing a "high-interaction" experience without running a real OS, comparing itself to Cowrie, which is a "medium-interaction" SSH honeypot

[THREAT MODEL]
Value: Sophisticated interactive attackers and post-compromise adversaries
Evidence: Sections I, VI-A, and VII-A:
LLMHoney is designed to engage "sophisticated attackers" who manually interact with the shell environment
. It specifically targets adversaries who execute "unexpected commands" or "uncommon flags" (such as docker or specific ls variations) and perform "sequential operations" that require high-fidelity, dynamic responses to prevent the attacker from realizing they are in a decoy

[IRB / ETHICS COMMITTEE]
Value: No
Evidence: A complete review of the paper confirms there is no mention of an Institutional Review Board (IRB) or ethical committee oversight

[CONSENT MODEL]
Value: None
Evidence: Section VII-B: "All experiments occurred in controlled laboratory conditions without real attacker interaction." Section IV-B: the only commands run against the honeypot were a researcher-curated "CSV of 138 Linux commands" used for benchmarking (commands.csv), not real attacker sessions.

[DATA TYPES COLLECTED]
Value: IPs, credentials, and commands
Evidence: Sec. III-E: Records "client IP/port," "Every authentication attempt and shell command," and the "LLM’s responses"

[PROTOCOLS & PORTS]
Value: SSH (Configurable; e.g., Port 8022)
Evidence: Sections III-A and III-B: The framework utilizes the "AsyncSSH" library on top of asyncio to open a TCP listener that handles the "SSH handshake (key exchange, and encryption)".
 The specific port is defined within the system's "Configuration Module" via a config.ini file, which allows the researcher to set a custom "TCP port for SSH", such as the Port "8022" used in the study's examples.

[DATA SENSITIVITY]
Value: Low
Evidence: Section III-E: "Every authentication attempt and shell command" and "The LLM's responses" are logged.
Section IV-B: "We made a CSV of 138 Linux commands spanning file, system, network, package, and utility categories. Each row in commands.csv provides: command: the exact CLI invocation. expected_output: the ground-truth response from a real shell."
Section VII-B: "All experiments occurred in controlled laboratory conditions without real attacker interaction."

[DATA MINIMIZATION]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Sec. III-E/Abstract: Emphasizes recording "Every... shell command" and interaction to "collect richer threat intelligence," with no mention of filtering or anonymization

[DATA RETENTION POLICY]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Sec. III-E: Logs are stored in an "on-disk database (honeypot_sessions.db)," but the paper provides no policy on storage duration or data deletion

[DATA SHARING]
Value: Not mentioned
Evidence: Section IV-A / Full Paper: While evaluating the system against "138 Linux commands," there is no specific mention of a public dataset release or open-source repository for the raw logs.

[JURISDICTION / LEGAL]
Value: Not discussed
Evidence: Despite deploying a system to capture real-time attacker data, there is no discussion of legal compliance, GDPR, or jurisdictional authority

[CONTAINMENT & HARM]
Value: Emulated sandbox
Evidence: Sec. VI-D: Safety is achieved because the "attacker is actually sandboxed—they cannot break out... there is no real shell, just the simulation"

[DECEPTION FRAMING]
Value: Purely technical
Evidence: Sections I, V, and IX: Deception is framed as a technical goal to overcome "fingerprinting" by providing "dynamic response generation for arbitrary commands" that traditional, static systems like Cowrie cannot handle
. The authors explicitly quantify the success of this deception using linguistic similarity metrics (BLEU-4, Cosine, and Jaro-Winkler scores) to measure how "convincingly" the AI-generated outputs mimic the ground-truth responses of an authentic Linux shell

[RESEARCHER RESPONSIBILITY]
Value: Implicit
Evidence: Abstract / Sec IX: Responsibility is framed as a contribution to gathering "threat intelligence" and moving toward "autonomous defense" through AI-driven security

[ETHICAL GAPS]

Evidence: zero discussion of the ethics of manipulating humans via AI, AI agency, or a formal data governance policy

[RELEVANCE TO PROJECT]
Value: AI-amplified deception / Ethics-by-Containment case study
Evidence: Sections VI-A, VI-D, and IX: This paper serves as a primary case study for the ethics-by-containment framework, representing the project's technology-driven ethical lag finding. 
It utilizes generative AI to reach high levels of deception realism while operating in a structural ethical vacuum specifically lacking IRB oversight and data governance policies. 
The authors explicitly rely on technical isolation "sandbox" as the sole substitute for normative ethical reasoning, which confirms the project's meta-finding on the field's dominant paradigm.
